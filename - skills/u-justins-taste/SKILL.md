# Justin's Taste

Central quality bar: Every aesthetic surface should feel polished enough to plausibly ship in Apple or Notion: disciplined, calm, usable, and finished across layout, motion, and states. Treat that as a craftsmanship benchmark, not an instruction to imitate either company's visual language.

## Purpose

Use this skill as a taste layer for visual design, UI polish, art direction, and frontend decisions. Guide the work toward design that feels restrained, spacious, precise, durable, and alive.

Treat this as a method of judgment, not a style recipe. Start with the product, content, audience, medium, constraints, and user-supplied material. Derive the visual language from those facts. Do not paste on whatever currently signals "premium" or mimic the surface traits of a reference.

Do not pursue minimalism for its own sake. Pursue quality through proportion, breathing room, hierarchy, material judgment, and responsive interaction.

## Taste Profile

Justin is drawn to design that feels calm, spacious, deliberate, and well-made without trying to announce itself as impressive.

The preferred feeling is quiet confidence. A page should feel like it has been architected with care: enough air around elements, clear relationships between text and image, measured typography, and a rhythm that lets the work or idea breathe.

Good design should feel composed before it feels decorated. The quality should come first from proportion, spacing, hierarchy, type choice, image placement, and restraint. Effects, motion, gradients, and visual flourishes are welcome when they deepen an already strong composition.

The result can be editorial, tactile, dynamic, dense, expressive, or image-led when the subject calls for it. Preserve its character. Keep it comfortable to look at and edited enough for repeat use; do not turn the interface into a performance or sand every project into tasteful neutrality.

## What Quality Feels Like

Quality often shows up as breathing room and proportion. Do not crowd important elements into the viewport or scale them until they become oppressive. Large type can be beautiful, but only when the page gives it enough space and the font has the right character. Let impact come from confidence and placement, not just size.

Build layouts that feel architected rather than filled. Make empty space intentional. A strong page can be quiet and still have presence.

Images should feel honestly placed. They should have a clear role in the composition instead of being forced to work as generic atmosphere. When imagery is variable or imperfect, the layout should still hold together through framing, cropping, spacing, and separation.

Study why a good reference works: margin, alignment, scale, type, image treatment, and clear roles. Transfer the underlying judgment, not its palette, typography, shapes, or fashionable surface cues.

Inspect optical alignment closely. Small accidental offsets across edges, baselines, gutters, control centers, and repeated components make restrained design feel unresolved.

## Composition And Specificity

Leave room for the thing being presented. Make interfaces, pages, and brand surfaces support the work, product, content, or decision rather than becoming the main event themselves.

Give text, imagery, controls, and empty space clear roles. If the page feels unresolved, first inspect proportion, hierarchy, spacing, crop, alignment, and type scale before reaching for more effects.

Use effects as finishing, not glue. Gradients, fades, overlays, blur, and motion should support a composition that already makes sense.

## Care And Non-Settling

A surface can be clean, functional, and still feel uncared-for. Justin is sensitive to that kind of absence: the feeling that a page was assembled from acceptable parts but never truly held, judged, and refined as a whole.

Do not merely avoid mistakes. Make care visible in proportion, hierarchy, spacing, type, image treatment, rhythm, and the way ordinary details resolve. Give buttons, metadata, settings, empty states, and secondary surfaces the same dignity as hero moments.

Do not stop at the first version that works. After a screenshot, render, or implementation pass, assume the result is still mediocre and ask what feels generic, unresolved, crowded, weightless, or emotionally absent. Improve the highest-leverage issue, then look again.

Welcome richness when it is edited. Make dense or fully populated surfaces feel composed, not stuffed. Restraint means every element has a reason, rank, and relationship; it does not mean removing life from the page.

## Typography Sensibility

Typography matters deeply to Justin's sense of quality, even though the exact preferred font direction is still emerging.

Type should feel measured, readable, and well-proportioned. Headlines should have confidence without becoming bloated. Body text should feel comfortable and human. The font should support the composition rather than become a novelty.

When a design feels wrong, type scale and font character are often worth questioning early. A smaller, better-spaced headline can feel more premium than a larger one that makes the viewport feel tight.

Look for type that holds its shape next to real imagery, supports generous spacing, and makes the page feel composed rather than inflated.

## Motion And Interaction Sensibility

Use motion when it feels like responsiveness: a soft answer from the interface. Do not add motion merely to satisfy this skill.

Subtle transitions, spatial continuity, gentle hover response, quiet depth, and proximity awareness can make a design feel alive. Motion should clarify selection, opening, closing, focus, cause, depth, or relationship.

The right motion is felt as polish rather than watched as an effect. It should be soft, reversible, useful, and comfortable after repeated use.

Push transitions, side panels, overlays, selected states, and image interactions should feel physically coherent: the interface makes room, responds to intent, and settles calmly.

## Design Review Questions

Do not ask merely "does this look designed?" Before approving a direction, ask:

- Does the page breathe without becoming empty?
- Does each element have a clear role?
- Is the hierarchy created by composition, not just size?
- Does the type feel measured and comfortable?
- Do the images feel honestly placed?
- Are edges, baselines, gutters, and repeated components optically aligned?
- Would this layout still work with less convenient content?
- Does this direction arise from the subject, or could it belong to any polished product?
- Are effects supporting the composition rather than holding it together?
- Does the result preserve character rather than sanding everything into tasteful neutrality?
- Does the interface feel quiet but alive?
- Does this merely work, or does it feel held and cared for?
- Would this still feel good after seeing it many times?
